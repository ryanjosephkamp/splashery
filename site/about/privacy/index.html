Privacy

Nothing you open leaves your device. No accounts, no tracking.

The short version

Nothing you open leaves your device. Splashery has no accounts, no tracking, no cookies and no ads. It is a set of plain files; the people who run it don't receive your files, your pictures, your microphone or camera, or a record of what you do.

Files you open

When you open a splat file, a photo, a PDF, a GIF, a video, a song, a 3D model or a data file, your browser reads it and turns it into splats on your device. It isn't uploaded to us or to anyone else. A link you share carries the toy and your settings, and for your own files only the settings (and a web address, if you chose one). The picture or file itself stays with you.

The microphone, the camera, the screen and your location

A toy asks for the microphone, the camera or screen capture only when you tap its button to start it. Nothing is requested before that, and nothing is recorded, stored or sent anywhere. The one exception is a recorder (the Sound lab's, for example): it records only when you tap Record, keeps the recording on your device, and saves it only to a file you choose. The Night sky can ask where you are only when you tap for it, and never stores or sends the answer.

The few times a toy reads something from the internet

Almost everything runs from files that ship with the site. A few toys can read open data, and only when you ask:

  • Earthquakes. The earthquakes toy reads the public earthquake feed of the U.S. Geological Survey when you open the toy or tap to refresh. A dated snapshot ships with the site for when the feed can't be reached.
  • Other open geographic feeds (animal tracking, migration) work the same way, and only when their terms allow reuse.
  • Protein structures. The molecule viewer can fetch a structure from the Protein Data Bank (CC0) by its code when you ask for it.
  • Wikipedia. The Wikipedia book fetches an article from Wikipedia when you ask for it. The text is never stored or shipped with the site, and its credit and license show beside it.

These use keyless public endpoints, show the source and the time of the data beside it, and store and send nothing about you. Your browser does ask those servers for the data, so they can see the request the way any website can; they don't learn anything else.

What stays in your browser

A few settings are kept in your browser's own storage, on your device only: the color theme, the Detail setting, the labs switch and, if you install Splashery, the files its offline copy keeps so toys you played work again without a connection. It is never sent anywhere, and clearing your site data removes it.

Where the site is hosted

Splashery's pages are served by GitHub Pages, the way any website is served. GitHub, like any host, can see ordinary web requests (an address and a time). Splashery adds no analytics or advertising of its own, and a toy loads nothing from third-party servers when you open it.
