About this toy

One cloud of Gaussian splats takes one form after another: words or names (emoji keep their colors), the Lorenz attractor, a Klein bottle, a gyroid, a loop, and QR codes. You set the list and its order. A tap morphs the cloud to the next form, starting where you tap and spreading from there; pick a form on the stage to go straight to it.

In the Toy tab, type a word to add it, add shapes or a QR code of the QR text, and move or remove forms. A QR code holds up to 42 bytes and scans once it settles. Drag through the cloud to push splats; they settle back. From Ryan Kamp's Overprint concept. Made by Ryan with Codex (GPT-6) and Claude (Opus 5.5); code is MIT, and the forms and sound are CC0.

Is it right?

Each form in the person's list is one sample of the same cloud of splats. The Lorenz equations, figure-eight Klein-bottle parametrization, gyroid approximation, and QR encoder have reference sources. Words are drawn with the device's own fonts; the loop is a graphic design; the list, flights, waves from a tap, pushes, colors, and the two-ink treatment are animation choices. Checked October 16, 2026.

  1. Correct The word form samples the typed text as the device's fonts draw it, emoji in their own colors.

    src/text-splats.js splits the text into grapheme clusters with Intl.Segmenter (so emoji sequences joined by zero-width joiners, flags, and skin tones stay whole), draws each one with canvas fillText in the system fonts, and samples its inked pixels with an R2 sequence. Color glyphs (emoji) keep their pixels' colors; other glyphs take the form's ink. Text is cleaned (spaces collapsed, control characters dropped), limited to 48 visible characters when added from the word box, and wrapped at spaces after ten characters per line. In Node, where there is no canvas, the site's five-by-seven bitmap glyphs stand in.

    The tests check grapheme splitting, determinism, and that every list form builds finite and inside the frame. How a glyph looks depends on the device's fonts, so the same word can look a little different on another device.

    • Intl.Segmenter, MDN Web Docs: The Intl.Segmenter object enables locale-sensitive text segmentation, enabling you to get meaningful items (graphemes, words or sentences) from a string.
    • CanvasRenderingContext2D: fillText() method, MDN Web Docs: fillText(), part of the Canvas 2D API, draws a text string at the specified coordinates, filling its characters with the current fill style.

  2. Correct The Lorenz form uses dx/dt = 10(y - x), dy/dt = x(28 - z) - y, and dz/dt = xy - (8/3)z.

    The derivative uses sigma = 10, rho = 28, and beta = 8/3. A fixed-step RK4 path uses step 0.007, discards 800 warm-up steps, and stores 18,000 points before display sampling, axis reordering, centering, and scaling.

    The test checks two independent derivative values and all three equilibria. This is a finite numerical trajectory, not weather data or a forecast.

    • Lorenz attractor, Wolfram MathWorld: The Lorenz equations are X' = sigma(Y - X), Y' = -XZ + rX - Y, and Z' = XY - bZ.
    • Lorenz system, Wikipedia: The commonly illustrated attractor uses rho = 28, sigma = 10, and beta = 8/3.

  3. Simplified The Klein form depicts a figure-eight Klein bottle with the expected self-intersections of a three-dimensional view.

    The existing Splat equation preset at time zero evaluates R = 2 + cos(u/2)sin(v) - sin(u/2)sin(2v), x = R cos(u), y = sin(u/2)sin(v) + cos(u/2)sin(2v), and z = R sin(u), for u and v from 0 to 2pi. The published figure-eight formula's y and z axes are exchanged; a display rotation and uniform fit follow.

    The reference states a > 2; the existing preset chooses radius parameter 2 for its graphic depiction. Tests check four reference points, not a proof of regularity or topology.

    • Klein bottle, Wolfram MathWorld: The figure-eight parametrization rotates and twists a figure eight, with radial factor a + cos(u/2)sin(v) - sin(u/2)sin(2v). A three-dimensional Klein-bottle picture is an immersion, rather than a self-intersection-free embedding.

  4. Simplified The gyroid form samples the trigonometric approximation sin(x)cos(y) + sin(y)cos(z) + sin(z)cos(x) = 0.

    Projected samples are accepted only when the implicit-field residual is at most 0.0001, then clipped to a ball of radius 2pi and fitted for display. The test undoes the display fit and checks sampled Float32 residuals within 0.00011.

    A small residual verifies the chosen trigonometric field, not exact minimal curvature. The display is a finite patch, not the infinite mathematical surface.

    • Gyroid, Wikipedia: The gyroid minimal surface can be trigonometrically approximated by sin(x)cos(y) + sin(y)cos(z) + sin(z)cos(x) = 0.
    • Gyroid, Wolfram MathWorld: The gyroid is an infinitely connected periodic minimal surface discovered by Schoen in 1970.

  5. Simplified The loop form is a decorative, flat three-node diagram, not a simulated orbit or a computed attractor.

    The sampler places a filled disk, two rings, straight connectors, and an upper arc at fixed two-dimensional coordinates. Splats move between this drawing and the other forms only through the shared animation.

    Its geometry and colors are graphic choices with no physical interpretation.

    • Shape morph's loop sampler, Splashery: The loop sampler draws disks, rings, connectors, and an arc with z fixed to zero; it does not integrate a dynamical system.

  6. Correct The QR form uses the vendored QR Code Model 2 encoder at error-correction level M and includes a four-module quiet zone.

    Selecting QR imports the shared encoder, calls it with level M and boosting disabled, and samples its dark and light modules with four blank modules on every side. The editable input accepts up to 42 UTF-8 bytes; a browser test submits 21 accented letters, exactly 42 bytes, and checks the resulting encoded text and level.

    The 42-byte limit is a display-budget choice, not the QR standard's capacity limit. The form's render tests also decode shorter messages; readability during a flight or from an oblique view is not guaranteed.

    • QR Code generator library, Project Nayuki: The library generates raw QR Code Model 2 modules, supports all four error-correction levels, and allows the caller to disable error-correction boosting.
    • Point for determining the code area, DENSO WAVE: A QR Code requires a clear four-module margin on all sides.

Where it simplifies

  • Every form is a finite splat sample, centered and scaled for the same camera; the splats are not a surface mesh.
  • The Lorenz trajectory is sampled from one fixed-step numerical path. Flights between forms and the spring-back push are animation choices, not Lorenz dynamics or material physics.
  • The Klein bottle is shown with self-intersections in three dimensions. Its radius, orientation, and colored bands are display choices.
  • The gyroid uses a trigonometric approximation to the minimal surface and clips it to a finite ball.
  • The word and loop are graphic designs (the word follows the device's fonts). The two-ink treatment changes colors, without simulating ink or printing.
  • A tap's morph starts at the tapped place: each splat waits 0.38 seconds for every unit it lies from the tap, then flies for 1.8 seconds. In flight the cloud wears its teal-to-pink ink, and a form's own colors come in as it lands. These are animation choices.
