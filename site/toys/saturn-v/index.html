About this toy

The Saturn V, the rocket that sent astronauts to the Moon from 1968 to 1972, as splats from NASA's 3D model. Its three stages were the S-IC with five F-1 engines, the S-II with five J-2s and the S-IVB with one J-2; the Apollo spacecraft rode on top under its escape tower.

Tap to stage it in the order of Apollo 11's flight, much faster than real: the first stage falls away at 2 minutes 40 seconds, then the ring below the second stage and the escape tower; the second stage at 9 minutes 8 seconds; the spacecraft leaves the third stage about three and a half hours after launch. The model paints a band of the first stage navy; the real one was white, so it is drawn white.

Is it right?

NASA's 3D model of the Saturn V, cut into its stages where the model's width steps, staging in the order of Apollo 11's flight, much faster than real. Checked October 5, 2026.

  1. Correct The pieces separate in the order of a real flight: the S-IC, the interstage ring, the launch escape tower, the S-II, then the spacecraft leaves the S-IVB.

    The drive moves each piece at its time on a 12-second clock; the test checks the order.

    • Saturn V, Wikipedia: First stage separation occurred less than one second after the engines were cut off ... At about T+3 minutes 12 seconds, the interstage ring dropped ... Shortly after interstage separation, the Launch Escape System was also jettisoned ... At about T+9 minutes 8 seconds ... separating the S-II ... About 40 minutes after TLI, the Apollo Command and Service module (CSM) separated from the third stage.

  2. Close The S-IC is the bottom 38% of the rocket's height (42 m of 110.6 m).

    The pieces are cut at shares of the model's height where its width steps, checked against the stages' lengths.

    The cuts follow the model, which is an artist's model rather than an engineering drawing.

    • Saturn V, Wikipedia: Height 110.6 m (363 ft); S-IC length 42 m (138 ft); S-IVB 58 ft 7 in (17.86 m) tall.

Where it simplifies

  • Staging that took hours is shown in 12 seconds.
  • The flames are glowing cones, not a simulation of the exhaust.
  • The model paints a band of the first stage navy blue; the real stage was white there, so it is drawn white.
  • The panels round the lunar module and the lunar module itself are not shown separately.
