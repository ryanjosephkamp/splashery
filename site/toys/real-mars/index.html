The real Mars labs

Tap a spot to zoom in and name it; tap again to go out. Play flies to Olympus Mons.

Tap
The real Mars as splats on its real elevation and color maps, turning at its real rate (scaled and labeled) and lit on the GPU by a sun the Sun slider moves; the relief can be exaggerated (labeled).
Sound
Space is silent: a soft breath as the view flies in.

About this toy

The real Mars from NASA and USGS maps: its colors from the Viking orbiters' mosaic, its heights from the Mars Global Surveyor's laser altimeter (MOLA), above the areoid, Mars's sea level. Each splat sits at its height and leans with its ground, so the sun lights the slopes facing it.

Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the solar system, rises about 21 km; Valles Marineris drops up to about 7 km; the Curiosity rover is climbing the mound in Gale crater. The relief is exaggerated ten times by default; pick True height in the Toy tab to see it as it is. A day on Mars lasts 24.6 hours; here an hour passes each second. The thin rim is its dusty air. A tap zooms in and names the spot from the IAU's list; tap again to go out.

Is it right?

Mars drawn from real maps: every splat sits at its place's measured height and has its place's measured color, and each named feature is where the planetary gazetteer puts it. The relief, the turning speed and the lighting are simplified, and each simplification is labeled in the toy. Checked October 7, 2026.

  1. Correct The color map is Mars Viking Colorized Global Mosaic 925m.

    tools/sp2-maps.mjs reads the source file named in src/space/worlds.js and averages it into the toy's smaller maps (east longitudes from -180° to 180°).

    • Mars Viking Colorized Global Mosaic 925m, NASA, JPL, Viking orbiters (Planetary Data System); USGS Astrogeology Science Center: Public domain (USGS and NASA).

  2. Correct The elevation map is Mars MGS MOLA DEM 463m.

    tools/sp2-maps.mjs reads the source file named in src/space/worlds.js and averages it into the toy's smaller maps (east longitudes from -180° to 180°).

    • Mars MGS MOLA DEM 463m, MOLA Science Team (NASA Goddard Space Flight Center); USGS Astrogeology Science Center: Public domain (USGS and NASA).

  3. Correct Mars turns once every 24.6229 hours (its sidereal day), sped up by the chosen factor.

    Each frame the drive adds the elapsed time times the speed-up, over the day's length, to the turn.

    • Mars Fact Sheet, NASA Space Science Data Coordinated Archive: Sidereal rotation period (hrs) 24.6229; volumetric mean radius 3389.5 km.

  4. Correct A tap flies to Olympus Mons at 18.65°, -133.8° east, and the map shows it there. Its summit on the map is over 20,500 m, within 60 km of the listed center (the map averages over cells about 1.6 km wide).

    The drive turns the feature's direction to face the viewer and glides the camera to it; the test checks the turn and the map's heights round the listed center.

    • Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature, USGS, IAU: Olympus Mons: diameter 610.13 km, center latitude 18.65, center longitude 226.20 (+East, 0 to 360).
    • Olympus Mons, Wikipedia: As measured by the Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter (MOLA), it is 21.287 kilometres (69,840 ft) high

  5. Correct A tap flies to Valles Marineris at -14.01°, -58.59° east, and the map shows it there. Its listed center lies more than 4,000 m below Mars's datum on the map.

    The drive turns the feature's direction to face the viewer and glides the camera to it; the test checks the turn and the map's heights round the listed center.

    • Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature, USGS, IAU: Valles Marineris: 3761.28 km, center latitude -14.01, center longitude 301.41 (+East, 0 to 360).
    • Valles Marineris, Wikipedia: more than 4,000 km long, 200 km wide and up to 7 km deep

  6. Correct A tap flies to Gale crater (the Curiosity rover) at -5.37°, 137.81° east, and the map shows it there. On the map, the mound rises more than 4,000 m above the crater's lowest floor.

    The drive turns the feature's direction to face the viewer and glides the camera to it; the test checks the turn and the map's heights round the listed center.

    • Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature, USGS, IAU: Gale: diameter 154.08 km, center latitude -5.37, center longitude 137.81 (+East).
    • Aeolis Mons, Wikipedia: It forms the central peak within Gale crater ... rising 5.5 km (18,000 ft) high from the valley floor

  7. Not yet checked A tap names the place under it: the smallest named feature it lies in, what that lies on, and the plain or highland round it, from the IAU Gazetteer.

    tools/sp3-places.mjs keeps each adopted feature center and size from the Gazetteer downloads; src/space/places.js finds the features a spot lies within (half their listed size from the center). tests/sp3.spec.mjs checks Tycho, Olympus Mons, Gale, Rachmaninoff, Maat Mons and Tranquility Base, and a tap on Tycho and on Olympus Mons in the browser.

    • Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature, IAU WGPSN and USGS Astrogeology Science Center

  8. Not yet checked Zoomed in, the ground comes from close-up tiles of the same maps, finer than the global map (the scales are in src/space/zoom.js); a feature named as a circle is an approximation of its real outline.

    tools/sp3-tiles.mjs averages each source into 10° tiles; tests/sp3.spec.mjs checks each world's tiles against its global map at a named feature.

    • Mars Viking Colorized Global Mosaic 925m, NASA, JPL, Viking orbiters (Planetary Data System); USGS Astrogeology Science Center: Public domain (USGS and NASA).

Where it simplifies

  • The relief is exaggerated by default (ten or twenty times; Pluto, Ceres and Vesta are drawn at true height), as chosen and labeled in the Toy tab; close up during a fly it eases to at most twice its height. True height is one choice away.
  • The world turns thousands of times faster than real (labeled in the Toy tab: an hour or a day each second).
  • North is always up and the sun stays near the equator's plane: the axial tilt and the seasons are left out.
  • The sun shades each slope by the cosine of its angle to the sun (Lambert's law) and leaves the night side dark, but nothing casts a shadow, and real surfaces scatter light in more complicated ways.
  • The global map has about 1,000 by 500 heights and 2,000 by 1,000 colors; only the patches round the named features are sharper.
  • The thin rim of air is a drawn glow, not a model of Mars's atmosphere.
