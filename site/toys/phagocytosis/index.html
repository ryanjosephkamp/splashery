About this toy

Phagocytosis means "cell eating". Tap and this neutrophil, a white blood cell, reaches out pseudopods around a bacterium until their tips meet, enclosing it in a bubble of membrane called a phagosome. Lysosomes, small sacs of digestive enzymes, fuse with it to make a phagolysosome, where the bacterium is broken down. The cell then releases the waste.

Neutrophils are the most common white blood cells; their nucleus has several lobes. A real neutrophil also kills with bursts of reactive oxygen; the toy shows the steps of eating.

Is it right?

The toy shows a neutrophil eating a bacterium in the steps a microbiology textbook gives: pseudopods wrap it into a phagosome, lysosomes fuse to make a phagolysosome, it is digested and the waste is released. The cell and its parts are a clear diagram. Checked October 5, 2026.

  1. Correct The phagocyte wraps the bacterium in pseudopods and pinches it off into a membrane vesicle, the phagosome.

    The membrane near the bacterium reaches round it in two layers until the opening closes; a phagosome around the bacterium is then drawn into the cell.

    • Microbiology, 17.4 Pathogen Recognition and Phagocytosis, OpenStax: the phagocyte forms a pseudopod that wraps around the pathogen and then pinches it off into a membrane vesicle called a phagosome.

  2. Simplified Lysosomes fuse with the phagosome to form a phagolysosome, where enzymes digest the bacterium.

    Five lysosomes travel to the phagosome one after another and join it; it turns pink (a phagolysosome); the bacterium comes apart into six pieces that shrink.

    The bacterium breaks into a few solid pieces; real digestion takes it apart molecule by molecule.

    • Microbiology, 17.4 Pathogen Recognition and Phagocytosis, OpenStax: The phagosome containing the pathogen fuses with one or more lysosomes, forming a phagolysosome … Lysosomal enzymes such as lysozyme, phospholipase, and proteases digest the pathogen.

  3. Correct The leftover waste is released from the cell.

    The phagolysosome moves to the membrane and its remains go out of the cell.

    • Microbiology, 17.4 Pathogen Recognition and Phagocytosis, OpenStax: Once degradation is complete, leftover waste products are excreted from the cell in an exocytic vesicle.

  4. Correct Neutrophils are the most common white blood cells, have a lobed nucleus and prefer to eat bacteria.

    The cell has a nucleus of three joined lobes.

    • Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 18.4 Leukocytes and Platelets, OpenStax: The nucleus has a distinct lobed appearance and may have two to five lobes … efficient phagocytes with a preference for bacteria.

Where it simplifies

  • The bacterium swims to the cell; in the body the neutrophil also crawls toward it, following chemical signals (chemotaxis).
  • The phagosome's acidification and the burst of reactive oxygen that neutrophils use to kill bacteria are left out.
  • New lysosomes bud from the Golgi and a new bacterium arrives at the end, so the toy can play again.
  • The cell is drawn about three times the bacterium's length; a real neutrophil (about 12 micrometers) is about six times a 2-micrometer bacterium.
