Real moons labs

Pick a moon in the Toy tab. Tap to fly to a named place and back. Move the Sun to relight it.

Tap
Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto and Titan from the USGS maps of Voyager, Galileo and Cassini pictures (no elevation maps exist, so the ground is smooth), turning at their real rates (scaled, labeled) and lit by a movable sun.
Sound
Space is silent: a soft breath as the view flies in.

About this toy

Five big moons from USGS maps of pictures by the Voyager, Galileo and Cassini spacecraft: volcanic Io; Europa, cracked ice over a hidden ocean; Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system; cratered Callisto; and Saturn's moon Titan, with lakes of liquid methane and ethane.

No spacecraft has mapped these moons' heights all the way round, so their ground is drawn smooth, in the moons' own colors (gray where the map is black and white). Titan's map was taken through its orange haze in near-infrared light, and the haze is left out. Seams show where the maps' pictures were lit differently. Each moon keeps one face toward its planet; here a day passes each second.

Is it right?

5 worlds drawn from real maps (Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Titan): every splat has its place's measured color, and each named feature is where the planetary gazetteer puts it. The turning speed and the lighting are simplified, and labeled. Checked October 5, 2026.

Where it simplifies

  • The world turns thousands of times faster than real (labeled in the Toy tab: an hour or a day each second).
  • North is always up and the sun stays near the equator's plane: the axial tilt and the seasons are left out.
  • The sun shades each slope by the cosine of its angle to the sun (Lambert's law) and leaves the night side dark, but nothing casts a shadow, and real surfaces scatter light in more complicated ways.
  • The global map has about 1,000 by 500 heights and 2,000 by 1,000 colors; only the patches round the named features are sharper.
  • Seams between pictures taken under different light show on the map.
  • Io has no global elevation map, so its ground is drawn smooth and the relief choice does not apply.
  • Europa has no global elevation map, so its ground is drawn smooth and the relief choice does not apply.
  • Ganymede has no global elevation map, so its ground is drawn smooth and the relief choice does not apply.
  • Callisto has no global elevation map, so its ground is drawn smooth and the relief choice does not apply.
  • Titan's thick haze is left out; its map was taken through the haze in near-infrared light, and seams between pictures taken under different light show.
  • Titan has no global elevation map, so its ground is drawn smooth and the relief choice does not apply.
