About this toy

Sand on a ringing plate gathers on the lines that stay still. The same happens in three dimensions, and labs use it to sort cells and tiny beads. Here a glass cell 1 cm across holds water and tiny plastic beads, and a transducer underneath fills it with ultrasound at one of the cell's resonances, so the sound stands still inside it. Each bead feels a gentle push, the acoustic radiation force, toward the places where the pressure hardly changes: the mode's nodal surfaces, the 3D cousins of a plate's nodal lines.

The toy works out that push from the Gor'kov potential for polystyrene beads in water (H. Bruus, "Acoustofluidics 7", Lab Chip, 2012) and moves every bead down it. The modes ring at about 100 to 370 kHz, far too high to hear, so you hear each one eight octaves down. A "mixed" mode blends modes that ring at the same pitch. Gravity is left out: beads this small sink only micrometers a second.
