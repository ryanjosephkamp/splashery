About this toy

Scientists share the 3D shapes of molecules as files of atom positions: proteins and DNA in the Protein Data Bank, small molecules as SDF, MOL or XYZ files. This viewer draws every atom of such a file with splats, in the usual colors: carbon gray, oxygen red, nitrogen blue.

Pick a sample, open your own file, or type a PDB code such as 1MBN and press Fetch in the Toy tab; the entry is read only when you ask, and its title, authors and fetch time show beside it. Draw it as a cartoon, balls and sticks, space-filling atoms or a smooth surface, colored by element, chain, residue or B-factor. Tap two atoms for the distance between their centers in ångströms, a third for the angle in degrees. Positions are an experiment's best fit, not a photograph.

Is it right?

The viewer draws the atom positions a structure file gives (PDB, mmCIF, SDF/MOL or XYZ), and measures distances and angles between atom centers from those positions. The readers are checked against known values: atom counts, the same structure read from two formats, crambin's peptide bonds, backbone angles and disulfide bridges, and caffeine's C=O bonds. The cartoon, the surface and the colors are drawings of that data; where they simplify, it is said below. Checked October 5, 2026.

  1. Correct Atoms are where the file puts them: the first model, the first alternate location, the same positions whether the structure comes as PDB or mmCIF.

    The PDB reader takes the fixed columns of ATOM and HETATM records; the mmCIF reader takes the _atom_site table. Crambin (1CRN) read both ways gives the same 327 atoms, elements, positions and B-factors.

    • Atomic Coordinate Entry Format Version 3.3, Coordinate Section, wwPDB: ATOM record, columns 31–38: orthogonal coordinates for X in Angstroms (Y and Z follow, then occupancy and temperature factor).
    • 1CRN: Water structure of a hydrophobic protein at atomic resolution, RCSB Protein Data Bank: Crambin, 46 residues, X-ray diffraction at 1.5 Å.

  2. Correct Distances are in ångströms and angles in degrees, between atom centers; crambin's peptide bonds measure 1.33 Å and its N–CA–C angles 111°, as standard protein geometry says.

    A distance is the straight line between two atoms' positions; an angle is taken at the middle atom from the two directions to the others. Across crambin's 45 peptide bonds the mean C–N length is within 0.01 Å of 1.329 Å, and the mean N–CA–C angle within 1° of 111.2°.

    The measured values are the experiment's model; the toy adds no uncertainty to them.

    • PROCHECK Operating Manual, Appendix A: Engh & Huber parameters, CCP4 (SLAC mirror): Main-chain bond lengths and bond angles as observed in small molecules (Engh & Huber, 1991): C–N 1.329 Å (σ 0.014); N–Cα–C 111.2° (σ 2.8).
    • Ångström, Wikipedia: One ångström is 10⁻¹⁰ m, 0.1 nanometer.

  3. Correct Bonds come from the file where it lists them, and otherwise from distances with standard covalent radii.

    SDF/MOL bond blocks and PDB CONECT records are read as given. Otherwise two atoms are bonded when they are closer than the sum of their covalent radii (Cordero et al. 2008) plus 0.45 Å, and a hydrogen keeps only its nearest partner. In crambin this finds exactly its three disulfide bridges (Cys 3–40, 4–32 and 16–26), each about 2.04 Å; caffeine read as XYZ gets the same 25 bonds its SDF lists.

    Distance rules can miss unusual bonds or join atoms that are only crowded; the Toy tab says which way the bonds were found.

    • Covalent radius, Wikipedia: Cites B. Cordero et al., "Covalent radii revisited", Dalton Trans. (21), 2832–2838 (2008), for single-bond covalent radii.
    • UniProt P01542, Crambin, UniProt: DISULFID 3..40, 4..32 and 16..26.
    • Disulfide, Wikipedia: The disulfide bond is about 2.05 Å in length.

  4. Correct Double bonds from an SDF file are drawn as two sticks; caffeine's two C=O bonds are 1.22 Å long.

    The bond order is read from the file's bond block; caffeine's ideal coordinates from the PDB's Chemical Component Dictionary give C=O bonds of 1.216 and 1.218 Å.

    • Carbonyl group, Wikipedia: For organic compounds, the length of the C–O bond does not vary widely from 120 picometers.
    • CFF, caffeine, RCSB Protein Data Bank (Chemical Component Dictionary): Caffeine, C8 H10 N4 O2, with ideal coordinates.

  5. Simplified Helices and strands come from the file's records; when a file has none they are worked out from backbone hydrogen bonds, and the toy says so.

    HELIX and SHEET records (PDB) or _struct_conf and _struct_sheet_range (mmCIF) mark the residues. Without them, a simplified DSSP finds backbone hydrogen bonds with Kabsch and Sander's electrostatic energy (below -0.5 kcal/mol) and builds helices from turns and strands from bridges, up to 3,000 amino acids. For crambin the file gives helices 7–19 and 23–30 and strands 1–4 and 32–35; with the records removed, the worked-out version agrees on more than three quarters of the residues.

    The worked-out version is a simplified DSSP (no 3-10 or pi helix types of their own, no turns or bends), labeled as inferred.

    • DSSP (algorithm), Wikipedia: A hydrogen bond is identified if E = 0.084 {1/rON + 1/rCH − 1/rOH − 1/rCN} · 332 kcal/mol is less than −0.5 kcal/mol.

  6. Correct Space-filling atoms are drawn at their van der Waals radii (Bondi), in the familiar CPK colors.

    Radii from Bondi (1964): H 1.20, C 1.70, N 1.55, O 1.52, S 1.80 Å and so on; Mantina et al. (2009) for main-group elements Bondi left out; 2.0 Å for elements with neither (the toy says so). Colors are Jmol's CPK colors, carbon a little lighter for the dark stage.

    Ball-and-stick balls are a quarter of the van der Waals radius, a drawing convention.

    • Van der Waals radius, Wikipedia: Hydrogen 1.2, carbon 1.7, nitrogen 1.55, oxygen 1.52, sulfur 1.8 Å: van der Waals radii taken from Bondi's compilation (1964).
    • CPK coloring, Wikipedia: Column J is the color scheme used by the molecular visualizer Jmol.

  7. Simplified The molecular surface is close to the surface a water molecule can touch, but smoothed.

    Each heavy atom adds exp(B(1 − d²/R²)) with R its van der Waals radius, so a lone atom's surface lies exactly at R; the surface is drawn where the sum reaches 1, sampled on a grid. Between crowded atoms the sum fills the gaps, as the solvent-excluded surface of a 1.4 Å probe does, but deep crevices come out shallower.

    Not a rolled-probe surface: an approximation, said in the Toy tab.

    • Accessible surface area, Wikipedia: The accessible surface is created by tracing the center of the probe sphere as it rolls along the van der Waals surface; the typical probe radius is 1.4 Å.

Where it simplifies

  • The cartoon is a drawing: a ribbon through the CA atoms (phosphates for DNA and RNA) with set widths, not a measured shape.
  • The surface is a blobby Gaussian surface over the heavy atoms, close to the solvent-excluded surface but smoother.
  • Big structures are drawn with fewer splats: one Gaussian per atom, and past the budget only the atoms on the outside (said in the Toy tab).
  • Only the first model and the first alternate location of each residue are shown.
  • A flat 2D drawing opened as MOL or SDF gets a 3D shape built by Splashery's embedder; its lengths and angles are estimates, and the toy says so.
