About this toy

A duoprism pairs two polygons at right angles in four dimensions: every corner of one is matched with every corner of the other. A 3,4-duoprism has 3 × 4 = 12 corners, and its walls are prisms. With two squares it is the hypercube. Each ring of the first polygon has its own color. It is drawn in perspective from 4D.

Tap it and it rolls a full turn through the fourth dimension and comes home. Pick each polygon, from a triangle to a hexagon, in the Toy tab. Source: J. H. Conway, H. Burgiel and C. Goodman-Strauss, The Symmetries of Things, 2008.

Is it right?

The p,q-duoprism, the product of a p-gon and a q-gon in 4D, with p and q from 3 to 6; all edges one length. Checked October 7, 2026.

  1. Correct A p,q-duoprism has pq corners and 2pq edges; with p = q = 4 it is the tesseract (32 edges).

    polytope() builds the corners from their coordinates and joins every pair at the shortest distance; a test checks the counts, the degrees and that every edge has the same length.

    • Duoprism, Wikipedia: A p,q-duoprism has pq vertices and 2pq edges; it is the set P₁ × P₂ of points (x, y, z, w) with (x, y) in one polygon and (z, w) in the other; the 4,4-duoprism is the tesseract.

  2. Correct It turns in four dimensions and is shown in perspective from a point out along the fourth axis.

    Each frame the corners are turned in the planes of x and w and of y and w, then projected by xyz × 2.4 / (2.4 − w); the edges are straight lines between the projected corners.

    • Tesseract (projections), Wikipedia: A 4D shape can be shown in 3D by perspective projection from a point outside it in the fourth dimension.

Where it simplifies

  • Only the corners and edges are drawn; the faces and cells are left out so the inside can be seen.
  • The view is a 3D shadow of the 4D shape, so edges can look different lengths and can cross.
