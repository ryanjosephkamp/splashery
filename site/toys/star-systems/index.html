About this toy

Real planets round other stars, from the NASA Exoplanet Archive: TRAPPIST-1, a cool red dwarf 40 light-years away with seven rocky planets, all closer to it than Mercury is to the Sun; TOI-178, whose six planets go round in a chain of near-simple ratios; and 55 Cancri, with a planet whose year is shorter than a day. The inner Solar System is there to compare.

The orbits' sizes and periods are measured, and the planets move at those rates, sped up. Star and planets are drawn far larger than to scale. Nobody knows these planets' colors, so they show their size: gray-brown for rocky, blue for mid-sized, tan for giants. Tap to see the system edge on, as we see TRAPPIST-1 and TOI-178: their planets were found as they passed in front of their stars.

Is it right?

Real planetary systems from the NASA Exoplanet Archive: each planet's orbit size and period are the archive's, and the planets go round at those rates, sped up as labeled. The star's and the planets' sizes are enlarged and their colors unknown, and the toy says both. Checked October 5, 2026.

  1. Correct The planets' orbit sizes, periods and radii are the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite values.

    tools/sp2-systems.mjs reads them from the archive's Planetary Systems Composite Parameters table through its TAP service.

    • NASA Science Data License, NASA: Data ... from a NASA-led mission ... are licensed as Creative Commons Zero. There are no restrictions on the usage of these data.

  2. Correct Each planet goes once round its star in its measured period (sped up by the chosen factor).

    The drive adds the elapsed days over each planet's period to its angle; the test checks the angles after 12 days.

    Kepler's third law holds within each system to better than 1%, except 55 Cancri e, whose orbit size the archive takes from a paper with another stellar mass (13%).

    • Planetary Systems table columns, NASA Exoplanet Archive: pl_orbper: Orbital Period [days], time the planet takes to make a complete orbit; pl_orbsmax: Orbit Semi-Major Axis [au]; pl_rade: Planet Radius [Earth Radius].

Where it simplifies

  • The star and the planets are drawn far larger than the orbits' scale (said in the toy).
  • Orbits are drawn as circles in one plane; the real ones are very slightly oval and tilted a little to each other.
  • The planets' colors are not known; they are colored by size class (said in the toy).
  • Each planet starts at an arbitrary place on its orbit, not where it is today.
  • Spread out (not to scale) places the orbits by the square root of their sizes.
