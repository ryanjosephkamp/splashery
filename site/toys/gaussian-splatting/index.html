About this toy

Gaussian splatting draws a scene with many soft, colored blobs called splats. Each one has a place, a size in three directions, a turn, a color and an opacity. A scan's splats are trained the way a network learns: start from a rough cloud of points (worked out from the photos, or random), compare the picture it makes with the photos, and nudge every splat a little, again and again, until they match.

Tap it: a cloud of random splats slides, stretches and recolors into the strawberry photo, a real fit of 2,400 splats, while its error falls on the chart. Other views show one splat with its three axes, which a tap trains: it starts from a wrong guess and gradient steps move its place, turn, sizes, color and opacity toward the target outline, big steps first and then smaller ones, as in a real fit; a duck whose splats shrink to dots, and the back-to-front order splats are drawn in.

Is it right?

The photo view uses a real optimizer on 2,400 flat Gaussians, with checked compositing and loss derivatives. It is a single-image 2D fit, not a multi-view 3D reconstruction. The one-splat motion is prepared, and the sorting view has a reproducible distance-versus-depth error. Checked October 4, 2026.

  1. Correct A photo is fit using 2,400 optimized Gaussians.

    The production fit count is 2,400. Independent pixel compositing agrees with the loss; finite differences agree with all 18 tested derivatives, and a small fit reduces its loss.

    • 3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering, Kerbl, Kopanas, Leimkühler, and Drettakis, Inria: The method optimizes 3D Gaussian parameters from multiple views, adapts the density of Gaussians, and renders them with visibility-aware splatting.
    • Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization, Kingma and Ba: Adam uses adaptive estimates of first and second moments of gradients.

  2. Simplified The training picture is a single-image 2D version of Gaussian fitting.

    It optimizes position, two sizes, angle, color, and opacity in a flat photo plane, using Adam and cached frames; it does not optimize full 3D covariance from multiple views.

    • 3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering, Kerbl, Kopanas, Leimkühler, and Drettakis, Inria: The method optimizes 3D Gaussian parameters from multiple views, adapts the density of Gaussians, and renders them with visibility-aware splatting.
    • Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization, Kingma and Ba: Adam uses adaptive estimates of first and second moments of gradients.

  3. Simplified The one-splat view illustrates optimization with a prescribed path.

    A shared factor (1−rate) to the step count reduces every target-parameter error for 24 steps, then forces zero. There is no rendered-image loss calculation in this view.

    This resembles gradient descent on a chosen quadratic parameter error; it is not another photometric fit.

    • Gradient descent, Dive into Deep Learning authors: Gradient descent moves against the gradient with a chosen step size; its convergence depends on the objective and step size.
    • 3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering, Kerbl, Kopanas, Leimkühler, and Drettakis, Inria: The method optimizes 3D Gaussian parameters from multiple views, adapts the density of Gaussians, and renders them with visibility-aware splatting.

  4. Being fixed The sorting view shows the renderer’s back-to-front order.

    The bead order is sorted by Euclidean distance to a fixed camera rather than view-axis depth. The test finds pairs whose order disagrees with depth.

    Distance order is not generally the transparency depth order for points away from the center of the view.

    • Gaussian rasterizer depth, Kerbl and coauthors, official implementation: The renderer uses the Gaussian center transformed into view coordinates and stores its z coordinate as depth.

Where it simplifies

  • The photo fit uses a reduced-resolution image and 2D parameters, caches its optimization, and replays keyframes. It does not infer a 3D scene from several cameras or adapt the splat count. The one-splat path follows prescribed parameter-error decay and snaps to the target at the end.

What is being fixed

  • Sort the illustration by signed camera-space depth, and keep its bead reveal and clicks synchronized with that ordering. (proposed)
  • Describe the one-splat view as a prepared parameter example, or give it an explicit loss and measured gradient in a future view. (proposed)
