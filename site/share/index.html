Embed and share

Put a live toy on your own page, with every option, or send someone a link to a toy exactly as you set it.

Try it: pick a toy and its options

Choose below. The toy updates, and the two snippets underneath are what you paste into your own page.

The iframe

Works on any page.

<iframe
  src="https://ryanjosephkamp.github.io/splashery/embed/?toy=strawberry"
  title="Splashery toy"
  loading="lazy"
  style="width:100%;max-width:600px;aspect-ratio:4/3;border:0;border-radius:12px"
></iframe>

The <splashery-toy> element

One script tag, no iframe. It starts when it scrolls into view and pauses when it scrolls away. 

<script type="module" src="https://ryanjosephkamp.github.io/splashery/src/element.js"></script>
<splashery-toy toy="strawberry"></splashery-toy>

Every option

The iframe takes options as a query string after embed/. The element takes the same options as attributes.

In the iframe's address On the element What it does
toy=cactus toy="cactus" A shelf toy by its id. Used when there is no scene.
#s=PAYLOAD scene="PAYLOAD" A scene from a share link: the toy with its settings, camera and look.
theme=light | dark theme="auto | light | dark" The color theme. The element's auto follows your page.
bg=transparent background="transparent | page | #rrggbb" A see-through or colored background.
autoplay=breeze autoplay="breeze" One gentle effect while it sits idle: breeze, pokes, twist or dissolve.
turntable=off turntable="off" Hold the toy still instead of turning it slowly.
zoom=1.5 zoom="1.5" From 0.5 to 2. At 1 the toy fills about 80% of the shorter side; 2 comes twice as close.
controls=0 controls="0" Hide the + and − zoom buttons.
label="A cactus" The accessible name read by a screen reader.

Good to know

  • Size. The iframe is 4:3 and fills its column, up to the width you set. A built-in toy loads well under 30 MB in all; even the heaviest scan is about 8 MB (the engine plus one file).
  • Transparent embeds. Add color-scheme: light to the iframe's style, as the snippet does, so browsers keep it see-through when a visitor's system is in dark mode.
  • Switching the theme from your page. Send the iframe a message: iframe.contentWindow.postMessage({ type: "splashery:theme", theme: "dark" }, "*"). The element follows the page by itself, including a paper-theme-change event on document and data-resolved-theme on <html>.
  • Scrolling and zooming. The mouse wheel scrolls your page until a visitor clicks or taps the toy; after that it zooms until the pointer leaves. Ctrl or ⌘ with the wheel, a pinch, and the + and − buttons always zoom.
  • Privacy. An embed loads nothing from third parties, shows no ads, and sets no cookies. Embeds are always silent.
  • The element's attributes also include scene (a share payload, see below) and label (the accessible name).

How scene links work

Every toy can be shared as a link. Open the gallery's Share tab and tap Copy link. The link looks like this: 

https://ryanjosephkamp.github.io/splashery/#s=PAYLOAD

The part after #s= is the scene: a small JSON document that says which toy it is, the camera, the look (background, theme, accent color, splat size and exposure), every effect's settings, any paint, and the pattern and motion. The JSON is compressed and written in a URL-safe form, so the payload starts with d. (compressed) or j. (plain JSON, for old browsers). It all lives after the #, so it goes to nobody: your browser keeps the part after a hash to itself.

  • Your own files. A link for a toy you opened yourself carries the settings only, and says so. Splashery doesn't put your file in a link. A link can carry a web address instead, so a picture hosted on your own site opens for whoever follows it.
  • Long scenes. If a scene is too long for a link (about 12 KB), the paint and then the clay edits are left out, and the app says so.
  • Old links keep working. Version 2 and 3 scenes, from any time, still load.
  • In an embed. The same payload works in the embed player: embed/#s=PAYLOAD, or the element's scene attribute. That is how an embed can show a toy with its exact look and camera.
  • As a file. Save JSON in the Share tab saves the same scene as a file, and Load JSON (or dropping the file on the page) opens it. The format is written down in the scene format.
