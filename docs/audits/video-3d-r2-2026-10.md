## Training and reproduction

The full-frame software WebGPU benchmark took 336 seconds for 12 steps at 960 pixels. A faster
256×256 native-pixel patch experiment lost held-out SSIM and was rejected. An optional D-SSIM
experiment exposed a missing camera-gradient storage binding; the isolated vendor fix, nine-buffer
device request, and four-step real-WebGPU regression prevent silent invalid training. Uploads keep
the existing browser-training defaults.

The accepted trainer is original MIT CPU/OpenMP code, build only, listed in `LICENSES.md`. It
optimizes positions, log scales, color, opacity, and optional quaternion rotation with full-frame
sorted Gaussian compositing, Charbonnier loss, Adam, high-gradient splitting, and low-opacity
pruning. The finite-difference test checks all 14 parameters on black and white backgrounds. It
excludes held-out frames and training frames substantially below the median sharpness.

`cdx-v3d-clarity.mjs` prepares immutable references, renders, and scores; `cdx-v3d-cpu-bake.mjs`
compiles and fits; `cdx-v3d-cpu-checkpoint.mjs` exports a real saved checkpoint with its hashes;
`cdx-v3d-cpu-score.mjs` provides quick checkpoint diagnostics. `cdx-v3d-clarity-pack.mjs`
round-trips the real payload, and `cdx-v3d-clarity-register.mjs` refuses stale, unmeasured, or
failing exports. `cdx-v3d-viewer-production.mjs` verifies the complete viewer.
`cdx-v3d-clarity-solve.py` reproduces or exports the native COLMAP solve. The numeric evidence and
frozen source hashes are in [the result record](video-3d-clarity-2026-10.json).

The native Mac route below remains available. It adds 60 original-resolution frames per capture,

shorter CPU continuations. Those Mac runs were not executed here, so their quality gain is not

## The fast check

`src/video3d/suitability.js` opens the chosen stretch locally and downsamples to 128 pixels. Four
short triplets cover 5%, 35%, 65%, and 95% of the shot. Adjacent frames in each triplet are at most
0.25 seconds apart. Local motion measures texture, blur, exposure, translation, spread, radial
expansion, unmatched patches, residual outliers, and direction reversals. Distant window centers
establish that the camera moved without mistaking long-baseline mismatches for shaking. The check
waits for the requested decoded frame to be presented before reading the canvas.

Risky or unknown results ask before training; phones always ask. The total cancellation budget
remains 4.5 seconds, with a one-second presentation wait inside it. The estimate cannot recognize
people or cars, prove a camera solve, or find every brief cut or shake. No model, trainer, upload,
external request, or WebGPU work is needed. The real-clip calibration appears below.

Explicit decoder failures reject the file and preserve the previously accepted clip. A decoding
timeout is qualified as unknown and can be retried. Changing the toy or selected source cancels the
old check or training confirmation, hides its card, and prevents stale results from replacing the
selected scene. Finish now still exports a partial genuine model when the selection is current.

Replay audio, its object URL, and its lens are released when the toy, source, player, or load
changes. Walk removes its controls as soon as its load loses ownership, even while the old scene
procedure remains on screen during preparation. Disposed hooks cannot move a later scene's camera.
Standalone embeds can adopt their player after the first build. Lens cleanup preserves a newer
scene's chosen field of view.

## Walking and cleanup

The walkable region is a connected 3D grid. Outside the recorded trajectory, a cell needs at least
two supporting camera views or 10% of the solved cameras, whichever is larger. A 32×24 depth map per
filmed view, built from opaque reconstructed splats, rejects cells behind visible surfaces and cells
with unknown depth. Recorded trajectory cells seed connectivity; isolated regions are discarded.
These are estimated geometric view counts, not unavailable training observation counts or an exact
collision mesh.

The grid covers camera bounds padded by 60% of a typical reconstructed surface distance, limited to
0.12–0.65 scene units. Its longest side has approximately 30 cells. Movement slides along supported
boundaries, subdivides steps to avoid skipping cells, and eases acceleration and deceleration.
Horizontal look permits a full turn; pitch stays below 80 degrees. Walking remains horizontal at the
selected camera height. Replay can still follow the recorded path, then hand its position back to
Walk. Frame-time steps remain capped at 0.05 seconds.

Walk and its Replay keep positions and Gaussian scales in the same affine frame as the cameras. The
legacy nonlinear contraction changes projections from translated cameras, so it is retained only for
Turn and zoom. A regression checks perspective ratios for distant points from multiple translated
cameras. Display variants have separate cache entries; changing navigation does not retrain a custom
result or change its original-video identity. Camera limits use the filmed path's bounds instead of
extreme distant splats. Walk also initializes after a standalone embed publishes its player.

Splat cleanup requires at least two estimated frustum supports or 6% of the filmed cameras. It
honors forward/up axes and roll. Both modes reject scales above 0.12 scene units and smears above
0.03 units with opacity below 0.4. One-axis rays compare their largest and middle scales, preserving
broad, thin surfaces. Upper background uses a stricter ray ratio of six; other geometry uses 24. A
small corridor around the original camera path is cleared and nearby scales are clamped
proportionally.

Both modes receive a soft dome when enough bright, sky-colored upper background samples exist. Those
samples supply its color; their classification never by itself removes geometry. The dome is larger
than the cleaned geometry, capped at 160 units within the unchanged 200-unit far clip. This is a
heuristic background treatment. Dark floaters still remain in Toolse, and Lihula's default desktop
Walk view remains visibly blurred. Cleanup cannot turn the compact software-GPU models into detailed
high-quality reconstructions.

The controlled regressions verify off-path movement through supported room space, unknown-space
boundaries, a full turn, and giant/smear/dome handling in both navigation modes. Actual browser
checks verify the stage camera and independent touch controls.

## Real-clip calibration

The quick check now samples four short triplets (12 frames) at 5%, 35%, 65%, and 95% of the shot.
Each local pair is at most 0.25 seconds apart. The old four-frame check compared frames six seconds
apart in a 20-second shot, beyond its eight-pixel patch search. Long-baseline centers now establish
that the camera moved; their unmatched patches do not diagnose local instability. The total
cancellation deadline remains 4.5 seconds. Files stay local, with no model, trainer, upload, or
WebGPU access.

Nearby motion gives translation, spread, unmatched patches, a radial expansion estimate, residual
outliers, and direction reversals. The decision remains an estimate: radial expansion can also be
forward travel, patch residuals can be depth or independent motion, and the check does not identify
people. More than five pixels of local movement plus inconsistent patches/direction reversals marks
a rapid, unstable pan; more than 10% radial expansion in one sampled pair marks a sudden
zoom/approach. Texture, blur, exposure, and little-motion checks remain.

diagnostic probe confirmed the canvas at requested 7.0 seconds still had exactly the 6.75-second
image hash, then changed at the matching requestVideoFrameCallback. checkVideo now waits for a
presented frame within 0.08 seconds of the requested timestamp, cancels the callback on abort, and
returns unknown on a bounded presentation timeout. Caller abort still throws AbortError.

## Native high-quality Mac bake workflow

`tools/cdx-v3d-mac-bake.mjs` implements `plan`, `prepare`, `solve`, `train`, `import`, and `bundle`.
Preparation was actually run for all four captures: 60 original-size PNG frames per 20-second shot,
no scale, crop, frame duplication, or JPEG recompression. It downloads the original Commons upload
using a separate ORIGINAL_CAPTURES map, verifies its exact byte count, SHA-256, video dimensions,
and every PNG’s dimensions, rechecks the live original-download link and license text, and records
the selected native frame timestamps and PNG hashes. It never reads the shipped 480p cuts as its
training source and refuses to write scratch data inside the repository.

| Capture   | Native capture dimensions | Source span | Prepared frames | Selected relative frame times |
| --------- | ------------------------- | ----------- | --------------: | ----------------------------- |
| Adjara    | 1080×1920                 | 0–20 s      |              60 | 0.007–19.677 s                |
| Lihula    | 1920×1080                 | 18–38 s     |              60 | 0.018–19.671 s                |
| Toolse    | 1920×1080                 | 14–34 s     |              60 | 0.003–19.683 s                |
| Põltsamaa | 2720×1530                 | 24–44 s     |              60 | 0–19.680 s                    |

The Mac tool imports titles, authors, licenses, and spans from historical CAPTURES, then overrides
only its local capture URL/hash/byte count/dimensions with ORIGINAL_CAPTURES. Historical CAPTURES
and the existing shipped short-bake hashes remain unchanged. All four original uploads are larger
than the transcodes previously used for the short browser bakes: Adjara increases from 270×480 to
1080×1920, Lihula and Toolse from 1280×720 to 1920×1080, and Põltsamaa from 1920×1080 to 2720×1530.
The actual original-upload preparation records and 240 full-size PNGs are now outside the repo at
`/tmp/cdx-v3d-mac-originals/<id>/`. The original downloads and FFprobe metadata are in
`/tmp/cdx-v3d-original-captures/verified-originals.json`; live-page fullMedia descriptions are in
`/tmp/cdx-v3d-original-pages/originals.json`.

Exact training settings: 30,000 iterations, no progressive downscales (`--num-downscales 0`), native
undistorted image size (`--downscale-factor 1`), SH degree 3, refinement every 100 steps after a
500-step warmup, densification gradient threshold 0.0002, and `--keep-crs`. There is no artificial
120,000-splat ceiling: msplat grows its representation automatically, and the actual exported count
is recorded. A full bake may contain millions of splats. Phone tiers must be made after visual
review of the full bake, with that reduction recorded separately.

The camera route is COLMAP 4.2.1, video mode, high quality, one SIMPLE_RADIAL camera, sparse
reconstruction only, and CPU feature matching (`--use_gpu 0`) so the Mac does not need CUDA.
Undistortion uses `--max_image_size -1` to preserve full resolution. The tool requires at least 54
of 60 images in one connected reconstruction before training; a fragmented or low-parallax shot
needs a different continuous span. This is a reconstruction sanity check, not a sharpness guarantee.

The msplat build is version 1.1.4 at Git revision `6b819711fa7c90f054567cb4fb4937743367afdd`, not
unpinned main. Its Python package requires Python 3.12 or newer. The pinned Python CLI exposes
`--num-iters`, `--output`, `--num-downscales`, `--downscale-factor`, `--keep-crs`, and the
densification flags used here. These flags were actually exercised through `--help` from the pinned
CLI source with tyro 0.9.35 in an isolated /tmp installation. The README's `-n` shorthand is not
exposed by that Python CLI, so the recipe uses `--num-iters 30000`.

The `--keep-crs` flag is essential. msplat internally centers/scales a COLMAP reconstruction; its
pinned PLY exporter restores the original world coordinates and Gaussian scales when this flag is
true. The import tool can therefore preserve the original COLMAP world-to-camera row-major R,t (x
right, y down, z forward), native f/fy/cx/cy/w/h, camera IDs, selected-frame timestamps,
calibration, and source hashes without guessing a transform. It records the actual PLY count, byte
size, completed iterations, and native training side. Its JSON includes stats.frames,
stats.registered, stats.iters, stats.plyBytes, bake.settings.trainSide, bake.exportTrainSide, and
backend=msplat/Metal for the registration tools.

The pinned exporter writes `comment msplat v<iteration>`; this is the completed step count, not the
package version. `python/bindings.cpp` passes current_step to Model::savePly, and
`save_gaussians.cpp` writes that number. The train stage requires a completed 30,000-step header and
a real PLY before writing a training record; import requires that record and a matching PLY hash.
The solve stage records camera/model/image hashes; changed camera metadata or training inputs are
rejected.

The current Splashery flight uses a centered, scalar-focal projection. Imported unequal fx/fy or an
off-center principal point is retained exactly in metadata, but may not replay pixel-perfectly until
the viewer consumes it. The toy's affine scene transform can carry the imported R,t consistently.
SIMPLE_RADIAL plus undistortion reduces this problem, but does not prove cx=w/2 and cy=h/2. Inspect
the recorded path and intrinsics before registering the result.

### Owner steps

Use an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 14 or newer, Homebrew, Git, and Xcode's Metal compiler. Open
Terminal on the Mac. Copy one command block at a time, and keep using the same Terminal window:
later steps reuse the variables and Python environment set earlier.

The times below are **planning estimates, not measurements on the owner's Mac**. Downloads, chip,
memory, and scene size change them; a stage can take longer. `/usr/bin/time -p` prints the actual
elapsed `real` seconds after each bake command. Preparation, solving, and training also record their
actual seconds in `preparation.json`, `solve.json`, and `training.json`.

1. **Check the Mac tools. A few seconds; once.**

   ```sh
   brew --version
   git --version
   xcrun -sdk macosx metal --version
   ```

   Continue when all three work. The Metal compiler check requires Xcode's Metal tools.

2. **Install dependencies. Allow 10–30 minutes; once.**

   ```sh
   brew install ffmpeg node python@3.12 cmake ninja \
     boost eigen openimageio curl libomp metis glog \
     googletest ceres-solver suitesparse qt glew cgal sqlite3
   ```

3. **Get this task's tools. Allow 2–15 minutes; once.**

   This creates a new checkout for the bake. Use a folder that does not already exist.

   ```sh
   V3D_REPO_DIR="$HOME/splashery-video3d-task40"
   git clone --depth 1 --single-branch \
     --branch codex/video-3d-r2 \
     https://github.com/ryanjosephkamp/splashery.git \
     "$V3D_REPO_DIR"
   cd "$V3D_REPO_DIR"
   npm ci
   ```

4. **Build COLMAP 4.2.1. Allow 10–40 minutes; once.**

   ```sh
   V3D_TOOLS_DIR="$HOME/splashery-video3d-build-tools"
   mkdir -p "$V3D_TOOLS_DIR"
   git clone --branch 4.2.1 --depth 1 \
     https://github.com/colmap/colmap.git \
     "$V3D_TOOLS_DIR/colmap-source"
   cmake -S "$V3D_TOOLS_DIR/colmap-source" \
     -B "$V3D_TOOLS_DIR/colmap-build" -GNinja \
     -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release \
     -DCMAKE_INSTALL_PREFIX="$V3D_TOOLS_DIR/colmap"
   cmake --build "$V3D_TOOLS_DIR/colmap-build" --parallel 4
   cmake --install "$V3D_TOOLS_DIR/colmap-build"
   export PATH="$V3D_TOOLS_DIR/colmap/bin:$PATH"
   colmap -h
   ```

   The last command must report version 4.2.1. An existing 4.2.1 installation can be used instead.

5. **Install the pinned Metal trainer. Allow 5–15 minutes; once.**

   ```sh
   V3D_TOOLS_DIR="$HOME/splashery-video3d-build-tools"
   mkdir -p "$V3D_TOOLS_DIR"
   python3.12 -m venv "$V3D_TOOLS_DIR/msplat-env"
   source "$V3D_TOOLS_DIR/msplat-env/bin/activate"
   V3D_MSPLAT_REV=6b819711fa7c90f054567cb4fb4937743367afdd
   python3 -m pip install 'tyro==0.9.35' \
     "msplat[cli] @ git+https://github.com/rayanht/msplat.git@$V3D_MSPLAT_REV"
   msplat-train --help
   ```

6. **Start with Lihula and prepare its pictures. Allow 2–15 minutes, plus picture review.**

   ```sh
   V3D_SCRATCH_DIR="$HOME/splashery-video3d-bakes"
   V3D_CAPTURE_ID=${V3D_CAPTURE_ID:-lihula}
   mkdir -p "$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR"
   cd "$V3D_REPO_DIR"
   node tools/cdx-v3d-mac-bake.mjs plan \
     "$V3D_CAPTURE_ID" "$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR"
   /usr/bin/time -p node tools/cdx-v3d-mac-bake.mjs prepare \
     "$V3D_CAPTURE_ID" "$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR"
   open "$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR/$V3D_CAPTURE_ID/images"
   ```

   Expect **60 original-resolution PNGs**, then `Prepared 60 native ... PNGs`. Look through all 60
   in Finder before continuing. They must show one continuous shot, with no identifiable people or
   logos. Keep the pictures as extracted: the next step checks their hashes.

7. **Solve the cameras. Allow 5–45 minutes per capture.**

   ```sh
   /usr/bin/time -p node tools/cdx-v3d-mac-bake.mjs solve \
     "$V3D_CAPTURE_ID" "$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR"
   ```

   Expect `Solved .../60 cameras`, with at least **54** placed. This includes full-resolution
   undistortion. The command can be quiet while COLMAP works. If a stage reports an error, send that
   error to the Operator; the later stages require its completed records.

8. **Train the full-resolution bake. Allow 15–90 minutes per capture.**

   ```sh
   /usr/bin/time -p node tools/cdx-v3d-mac-bake.mjs train \
     "$V3D_CAPTURE_ID" "$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR"
   ```

   Keep Terminal open until it prints `Trained 30000 steps and exported ... splats`. Progress goes
   into `$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR/$V3D_CAPTURE_ID/training.log`; the command can be quiet. The tool uses
   30,000 iterations, the native undistorted resolution, 60 selected frames, and `--keep-crs`.

9. **Make the camera pair and return archive. Allow 1–5 minutes per capture.**

   ```sh
   /usr/bin/time -p node tools/cdx-v3d-mac-bake.mjs import \
     "$V3D_CAPTURE_ID" "$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR"
   /usr/bin/time -p node tools/cdx-v3d-mac-bake.mjs bundle \
     "$V3D_CAPTURE_ID" "$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR"
   open -R "$V3D_SCRATCH_DIR/$V3D_CAPTURE_ID-return.tar.gz"
   ```

   Send **`lihula-return.tar.gz`** to the Operator through the existing private handoff. It contains
   `lihula.ply`, `lihula.json`, `preparation.json`, `cameras.json`, `solve.json`, `training.json`,
   `training.log`, `extraction.log`, and the three COLMAP text files in `undistorted-text/`
   (`cameras.txt`, `images.txt`, and `points3D.txt`). The bundle checks the completed budget, PLY
   hash, and retained source license. It includes only those files. Keep the original movie and
   prepared pictures on the Mac so the Operator can request them for further review.

10. **Repeat steps 6–9 for the other three captures.**

    Change only the capture variable, using one ID at a time:

    ```sh
    V3D_CAPTURE_ID=adjara
    ```

    Then use `toolse`, and then `poltsamaa`. Their return files are **`adjara-return.tar.gz`**,
    **`toolse-return.tar.gz`**, and **`poltsamaa-return.tar.gz`**. Each capture has its own scratch
    folder. Lihula can be sent and installed first; the other three do not have to be finished yet.
    Toolse and Põltsamaa pictures, models, and derived tiers retain **CC BY-SA 4.0**.

### Installing returned bakes (Operator)

Review the full model, recorded path, artifacts, native intrinsics, and source-derived credit before
registering a return. On the task branch, extract a reviewed archive into its capture folder:

```sh
V3D_RETURN_DIR="$HOME/splashery-video3d-reviewed"
mkdir -p "$V3D_RETURN_DIR/lihula"
tar -xzf "$HOME/Downloads/lihula-return.tar.gz" \
  -C "$V3D_RETURN_DIR/lihula"
node tools/cdx-v3d-register.mjs "$V3D_RETURN_DIR" lihula
```

Repeat with the other IDs as their bakes are reviewed. Omitting the final ID registers all four when
all four pairs are present. The registration tool builds BKD1 tiers, updates the selected camera
JSON, regenerates its sample descriptor and hashes, and refreshes source credits. It leaves large
raw PLYs in the build folder and applies the default-byte gate after compaction.

The existing toy reads the replacement at the same sample ID and manifest path; **no manual viewer
or training code change is needed**. Reload the toy in a fresh browser context and select the sample
to review the new data. Existing saved scenes and share links keep their source IDs. The regression
fixture registers a large replacement, loads it through the unchanged scene loader, then installs a
separate single-sample return without needing the earlier large PLY again. This proves the
replacement contract, not a native quality gain; these are packaging/registration fixtures, not new
trained scenes.

The msplat README's full-resolution benchmark on an M4 Max is about 70–80 seconds for 7,000 steps;
its 30,000-step Garden benchmark is about 700 seconds and 3.51 million Gaussians. Those are upstream
measurements of a different scene, not a timing or quality result for these four captures. The old
lab page conflates the short and long budgets. Even 30,000 native-resolution steps cannot supply
missing views, fix wrong poses, or turn a moving scene into a static one.
