# Video to 3D r2: a world along the filmed path

October 12, 2026. Built by Codex (GPT-6), on `codex/video-3d-r2`, from `main` at `16f23e80`. Rule
check: none.

The branch also includes `main` through `16f23e80`. Four new captures were genuinely solved and
trained on software WebGPU. Compact initial bakes and higher-resolution finishing passes are
recorded separately in their metadata. These are mixed-resolution models, rather than full
3,000-step high bakes from scratch.

## What changed

- A small local suitability check appears before the trainer loads. Risky or uncheckable clips ask
  before training, and phones always ask. The filming guide explains the phone's 16-frame setting
  and eight-minute training limit.
- Walk mode uses keys and a mouse drag on a desktop, or two independent thumb pads on a phone.
  Movement eases, stays near the solved camera path, and stops at its ends. Looking is limited to
  the directions the video recorded. Back to start returns to the first camera.
- Cleanup estimates which cameras could see a splat, removes stretched rays and near-camera
  floaters, and limits splat sizes along the path. Turn and zoom adds a soft upper dome using colors
  from the trained scene's distant upper splats.
- Short runs can refine and grow splats before their step budget ends. The vendor change is in its
  own commit, `ffcc71cc`, with the notice in `LICENSES.md`. The existing improving training preview
  remains, with smaller submission batches and a shallower queue on phones.
- The stretch picker now includes 60 and 90 seconds. These remain experimental: the same frame
  budget samples a longer stretch more thinly, and the upload limit is still 40 MB.

Only the task's Video to 3D files, tools, tests, samples, credit entries, this report, and the
separate permitted vendor change are included. Other Studio toys keep their own files.

## The fast check

`src/video3d/suitability.js` opens the chosen stretch locally, copies four frames at a maximum of
128 pixels on the long side, and checks:

| Signal                                    | Interpretation and advice                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Grayscale contrast and textured patches   | Blank walls, sky, or water give too few details to match. Include textured surfaces. |
| Laplacian variance                        | Soft or blurred frames need steadier movement and better light.                      |
| Average brightness across frames          | Exposure changes need even light and a fixed exposure.                               |
| Brightness-normalized local patch matches | Very little displacement suggests a stationary camera.                               |
| Differences between patch displacements   | Different motion suggests depth; uniform motion may be a pan.                        |
| Unmatched patches                         | A cut, fast motion, reflections, or moving objects may interfere.                    |

There is a 4.5-second cancellation budget. Unsupported or slow decoding returns an explicit "Could
not check this video quickly" result instead of silently beginning a long reconstruction. The
estimate is deliberately qualified: it cannot recognize people or cars, prove a valid camera solve,
or distinguish every pan from translation. The check uses local picture statistics. No model,
trainer, upload, or external request is needed for the check.

Explicit decoder failures reject the file and preserve the previously accepted clip. A decoding
timeout is qualified as unknown and can be retried. Changing the toy or selected source cancels the
old check or training confirmation, hides its card, and prevents stale results from replacing the
selected scene. Finish now still exports a partial genuine model when the selection is current.

Replay audio, its object URL, and its lens are released when the toy, source, player, or load
changes. Walk removes its controls as soon as its load loses ownership, even while the old scene
procedure remains on screen during preparation. Disposed hooks cannot move a later scene's camera.
Standalone embeds can adopt their player after the first build. Lens cleanup preserves a newer
scene's chosen field of view.

## Walking and cleanup

The walkable region is a tube around consecutive solved cameras. Its radius is 35% of the median
camera spacing, limited to 0.012–0.055 scene units. Speed follows the camera spacing, with
acceleration and deceleration eased over time. The lens matches the filmed field of view and adapts
to the stage's size. Movement steps are capped at 0.05 seconds so returning to a backgrounded tab
cannot teleport the viewer. Horizontal look stays within 0.35 radians of the nearest recorded
direction; vertical look stays within 0.5 radians. This is a bounded exploration of captured views,
without a claim of complete walls, collision geometry, or a reconstructed world beyond the footage.

Walk and its Replay keep positions and Gaussian scales in the same affine frame as the cameras. The
legacy nonlinear contraction changes projections from translated cameras, so it is retained only for
Turn and zoom. A regression checks perspective ratios for distant points from multiple translated
cameras. Display variants have separate cache entries; changing navigation does not retrain a custom
result or change its original-video identity. Camera limits use the filmed path's bounds instead of
extreme distant splats. Walk also initializes after a standalone embed publishes its player.

Cleanup requires at least two estimated camera-frustum supports, or 6% of the solved views,
whichever is larger. It honors the camera's actual forward and up axes, including roll. Splat.js
does not export observation counts or an occlusion map in its PLY: these are possible views, not
proof that each frame observed a splat. The legacy contracted far shell is exempt; affine walking
tests every splat for camera support. Absolute giant/smear cutoffs are skipped for affine geometry,
where valid distant surfaces can be large in scene units. A ray has one long axis and two short
ones: its largest scale is compared with the middle scale, preserving broad, thin surfaces.
Extremely stretched splats are removed, and a 0.012-unit corridor around the camera path is cleared.
Nearby splats have their scales limited proportionally to their path distance.

The dome averages distant upper splat colors from the video reconstruction and adds a small horizon
gradient. It is only built when enough such splats exist, and only for the contracted display.
Affine walking retains its reconstructed background: the fixed-radius dome could hide actual distant
geometry, and the export cannot reliably distinguish sky from mountains or buildings. The dome is a
background treatment, not a semantic sky mask or recovered geometry. Missing surfaces, moving people
in the old Street sample, and soft texture remain visible.

The container has a software WebGPU adapter, not a hardware GPU. Initial bakes use 24 solved frames,
up to 800-pixel feature inputs, 256-pixel training frames, a 30,000-splat ceiling, and a 20-minute
budget. Higher-resolution finishing reuses the learned model and the exact recorded frame times, at
640 pixels on the long side. It verifies the actual image dimensions and scaled intrinsics, restores
opacity at the original training focal length, and disables refinement and relocation during the
short finishing pass. This prevents a final step from inserting untrained points. Initial bake
hashes, native source cut hashes, actual training-camera intrinsics, completed steps, elapsed time,
and continuation history are retained in each scene's JSON.

The forest's downloaded source is only 270×480. Its 640-pixel inputs are explicitly marked as
upscaled, without a claim of new source detail. The Lihula and Toolse captures are 1280×720;
Põltsamaa is 1920×1080. The forest's matched first-camera image PSNR rose from 18.71 to 18.99 dB
after finishing, a modest improvement. Lihula's first-camera result changed from 23.91 to 23.75 dB:
this does not support a sharpness improvement. PSNR is measured on recorded views, not a novel-view
quality score. Higher input resolution does not guarantee a visibly better model.

Toolse's matched first-camera PSNR rose from 24.38 to 25.40 dB, with modestly clearer castle
contrast. Lihula's middle and last cameras changed from 26.62 to 26.74 and 24.22 to 23.79 dB,
respectively. Those views stayed coherent but almost identical. Their finishing pass is recorded as
actual higher-resolution conditioning, without a claim of visible improvement overall.

Põltsamaa's matched first-camera PSNR rose from 25.84 to 26.08 dB. The church, courtyard, roofs,
river bend, and bridge remain recognizable and coherent; fine foreground detail remains soft. Each
accepted model completed 102 additional steps with actual 640-pixel training images.

Thumbnails were rerendered, their contact sheet was reviewed, and the new review thumbnail was
retained separately. Standard screenshots and the existing thumbnail were restored as required by

and a broader smoke attempt were run. `npx prettier --check .`, `node tools/us-english.mjs --diff`,

The new tests cover pure suitability signals, actual local decoding of a useful clip and a blank
clip, preservation of an accepted file after a decoder error, confirmation cancellation, scene
ownership during loading, camera perspective, path bounds and easing, actual desktop movement,
independent phone pointers, every shipped sample, hashes, and standalone ShareAlike attribution.
Some lifecycle checks stub GPU availability and use small camera/player shells; the audio cleanup
check uses an Audio stand-in and real Blob object URLs. The custom navigation cache test stubs the
expensive training call with an existing genuine export. A separate scheduling test stubs GPU work
while exercising the real Session loop. The five attempted captures were genuinely solved and
trained; four are selected for shipping. Screenshots, clips, sample loading, desktop movement, and
the two-thumb tests use the actual browser renderer. No synthetic reconstruction is counted as a
bake.

A broader smoke run exposed a rig/effect-reset pixel assertion that also fails on unchanged `main`
at `16f23e80`. The isolated baseline run can be repeated with
`npx playwright test tests/smoke.spec.mjs --grep='shelf shapes have rigs'`. It received 82,306
changed pixels against an expected value below 14,038.5. This unrelated failure was not changed
within the Video to 3D task's scope. Two other failures during competing software-GPU work passed
when rerun separately: the suitability timing check and a procedural-preview smoke check. The
4.5-second suitability deadline returns a qualified unknown result when decoding is too slow.

The main limits remain: possible false suitability estimates, missing geometry outside the video's
views, soft compact bakes, a heuristic sky treatment, and a camera corridor rather than physical
collisions. Longer 60- and 90-second training stretches were enabled but have not yet been measured
as complete reconstructions. High baked sample tiers and a ground-level indoor room capture are not
included in this software-GPU run.
