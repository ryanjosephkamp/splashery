# Element facts: a proposal

Checked on October 2, 2026. Two short facts for each of the 118 elements, in atomic-number order.
This is a content proposal; no periodic-table code changes are included. Room-temperature
descriptions refer to the pure element under ordinary conditions. Uses of compounds are identified
as such. For the heaviest elements, a few synthesized atoms do not establish the appearance or state
of a bulk sample. Discovery accounts can involve competing claims; the wording below follows the
cited source rather than inventing a single discoverer.

The Royal Society of Chemistry pages for all 118 elements were opened. Its speculative appearance
descriptions for the heaviest elements are deliberately not used. IUPAC supplies the final naming
rationale for elements 113, 115, 117 and 118.

## 1. Hydrogen (H)

- The lightest element is an invisible gas at room temperature.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Hydrogen](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/1/hydrogen)
- Industry combines hydrogen with nitrogen to make ammonia for fertilizer.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Hydrogen](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/1/hydrogen)

## 2. Helium (He)

- Helium is a colorless gas that is much lighter than air.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Helium](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/2/helium)
- Liquid helium keeps the powerful magnets in MRI scanners extremely cold.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Helium](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/2/helium)

## 3. Lithium (Li)

- Lithium is a soft metal light enough to float on water, although it reacts with it.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Lithium](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/3/lithium)
- Lithium compounds are central to rechargeable batteries in portable electronics.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Lithium](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/3/lithium)

## 4. Beryllium (Be)

- Beryllium is a lightweight gray metal.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Beryllium](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/4/beryllium)
- It transmits X-rays well, making it useful for X-ray equipment windows.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Beryllium](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/4/beryllium)

## 5. Boron (B)

- Pure boron can appear as a dark powder.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Boron](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/5/boron)
- Boron compounds help make heat-resistant glass.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Boron](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/5/boron)

- Diamond and graphite are different structures made from the same element, carbon.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Carbon](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/6/carbon)
- Graphite conducts electricity, while diamond is an electrical insulator.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Carbon](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/6/carbon)

## 7. Nitrogen (N)

- Nitrogen gas makes up about four fifths of the air around us.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Nitrogen](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/7/nitrogen)
- Liquid nitrogen provides intense cooling for preserving biological samples.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Nitrogen](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/7/nitrogen)

## 8. Oxygen (O)

- Oxygen is a colorless gas; when liquefied it is pale blue.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Oxygen](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/8/oxygen)
- Steelmaking uses large amounts of oxygen to remove unwanted carbon from iron.
  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Oxygen](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/8/oxygen)

## 9. Fluorine (F)

  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Fluorine](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/9/fluorine)

  [Royal Society of Chemistry: Fluorine](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/9/fluorine)

- Thallium is a soft metal that tarnishes quickly in air.
