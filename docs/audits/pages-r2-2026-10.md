## Comparison and method

The baseline is `main` at `cc297c89e`, including Fix13 (#665), served from an isolated, unchanged
checkout. The implementation is on `codex/book-picture-lab-r2`. Engine work is committed separately.
The comparison tool is `tools/pages-bench.mjs`, with `tools/pages-bench-report.mjs` for the strict
comparison. Final measurements pair main and the candidate next to each other for each input and
repeat. Repeat 2 reverses their order. Each case starts a fresh Chromium process. For example, the
first baseline case is:

```sh
DISPLAY=:91 SPLASHERY_GL=llvmpipe SPLASHERY_CHROMIUM=/usr/bin/chromium \
  node tools/pages-bench.mjs --url=http://127.0.0.1:4180/ --profiles=phone \
  --toys=picture-lab --cases=photo --repeats=1 --seconds=15 \
  --out=work/pages-r2/paired-photo/1-main
```

Run its candidate against port 4173, repeat in reverse order, then repeat in the first order. Use
the same schedule for every toy, input, and profile. The final records contain three repeats per
group, with both full tool and measurement-protocol hashes. Run from the implementation checkout,
and serve both checkouts with the same cached `work/pages-r2/fixtures` bytes; the isolated baseline
uses a symlink to that directory.

Earlier runs measured all of main before the candidate. Identical code then produced substantially
different loading times and frame rates between runs, so those separated timing comparisons are
exploratory. The final adjacent pairs reduce that time separation while retaining all three repeats
and the original thresholds. An interrupted exploratory chart group restarted in full, and a
full-page review screenshot exceeded its default 30-second timeout. The artifact timeout is now 180
seconds; the measurement function, fixture bytes, and quality calculations were unchanged by that
timeout fix. Final pairs use the same tool version and Mesa renderer throughout. Earlier SwiftShader
runs remain exploratory; both revisions were remeasured after the renderer changed. Each final
record includes the actual WebGL backend and unmasked driver string, rather than relying on launch
flags.

The matrix opens all three toys with their shipped samples, an original text-heavy PDF, a photo, and
an original chart. The independent photo reuses the Picture frame's credited CC0 sample; it is not a
newly licensed asset. The text and chart are original CC0 benchmark material. Image inputs become
PDF pages for Your book, and the text page becomes a still image for the album. The generated
fixtures are archived on the evidence-only branch, with no visitor data or new external downloads.

Phone: 390×844 CSS pixels, device scale 2, low detail, adaptation off. Desktop: 1440×900, device
scale 1, mid detail, adaptation off. These are real Chromium frames on Mesa llvmpipe (LLVM 19.1.7)
through ANGLE in Linux, not measurements from a physical phone. The low tier retains its normal 1.5
pixel-ratio cap; quality is measured on the actual rendered framebuffer. Main and the candidate run
sequentially. Early exploratory runs used intermediate camera and crop registration; they are
excluded from the final comparison. In particular, an album opening turn could outlast its camera
glide, and a slow GPU could leave the old camera matrix in the stage after the requested camera
angles changed. Final captures wait for landed poses and explicitly complete a draw before
projecting the reference crop. The final memory snapshot is taken before allocating the independent
reference raster and screenshots. Earlier exploratory memory readings included those tool-only
allocations and reused a native browser GPU process across cases; those readings are excluded from
the final footprint comparison.

Time to first page starts before choosing the sample toy, or before opening the custom input once
the toy is chosen. The final comparison records installation separately and then waits for three
completed page draws, so the first sort and texture upload are included. Background preparation does
not have to stop. Still captures wait for the recipe's opening or page turn to land, all requested
sheets to match for at least 600 milliseconds, and the normal reading-view glide and camera to
settle; the book's page motion is then frozen for crop registration. Turn timing runs on the real
clock, counts `postrender` events, requests rendering throughout 15 seconds, and issues two taps. It
reports rendered frames per second, not update ticks or completed page turns per second. Shipped
multipage samples exercise navigation; single-page and single-image fixtures measure active
rendering during their available tap or cover action.

The source reference is rendered independently at width 2,048, rather than using the candidate's
raster resolution. Its crop follows the sheet's actual leaf, part, body, and entity transforms,
including the colored face's offset from the backing. Front-facing captures keep the actual
framebuffer pixels and paint the source at the same fractional projected bounds; there is no extra
resampling of the sharper rendering. The text fixture's body crop is `[0.08, 0.20, 0.92, 0.60]`.
Other inputs use `[0.12, 0.25, 0.88, 0.60]`. There is no search for a favorable image shift.
Sharpness is variance of the four-neighbor luminance Laplacian; SSIM uses local 8×8 luminance
windows, K1 = 0.01, and K2 = 0.03. Higher sharpness alone is insufficient: SSIM and the rendered
crops must also improve without visible noise. The primary edge measure is edge preservation:
rendered Laplacian variance divided by the source's variance at the displayed size. Raw variance is
retained too, and the phone text check also requires its raw variance to improve by at least 30%.
This accounts for the small perspective-size change when ink moves from its old floating splat plane
onto the continuous face. The comparison rejects views whose projected dimensions differ by 2% or
more.

Memory records include JavaScript's memory estimate, CDP heap size, explicit sheet buffers and
source canvases, and the renderer's resource counters. Browser and software-renderer overhead are
separate from sheet data. Measurements and final comparisons will be recorded below after the matrix
completes. Linux runs also record proportional resident size across Chromium's processes, including
native allocations and the software GPU, without counting shared pages repeatedly.

## Implementation

- An opt-in continuous picture face sits over the existing Gaussian paper and binding. It uses the
  same circular-arc leaf equation and rigid part transforms in both WebGL2 and WebGPU. Shared
  vertices and opaque depth writes keep each face connected throughout a turn.
- PDF paper retains coarse splats and edge bands, without allocating a full hidden raster of ink
  splats. Picture lab photos retain alternating Gaussian color samples under the sharp face. On the
  low tier, that hidden photo backing uses half the old pixel allowance; the sharp face retains its
  full resolution. Mounted album photos use coarse paper backing, saving the duplicate detail work
  beneath their opaque faces. The Gaussian backing keeps its original pixel budget, and the face's
  texture has a separate bound.
- Documents render at 1.5 times their projected width within the existing width cap, over a Gaussian
  backing at one-quarter of the source width. A one-level texture bias avoids filtering already
  antialiased letters twice, while mipmaps and anisotropic sampling remain available as a page
  turns.
- Strictly opaque grayscale pages store each original 8-bit value in one texture channel. The
  fragment shader repeats that value into RGB without quantization. A full-raster check preserves
  even tiny colored marks and transparent pixels through the full-color path. Two-color ink uses a
  coverage channel only when every source channel can be reconstructed within one 8-bit step;
  unrelated colors and transparency retain RGBA.
- The first face installs before a moving opening camera requests its next resolution; normal
  settled-view upgrades then provide full detail. This avoids discarding the first readable image.
- Source color compaction overlaps the paper worker, using independent pixel buffers instead of
  serializing both jobs before installation.
- Native-image faces upload exact RGBA bytes and release their staging canvas. The cache keeps only
  the latest resolution of each native image, instead of duplicate Gaussian buffers for the
  intermediate sizes crossed during an opening glide.
- Flat, rigid faces use one quad and a shorter vertex shader. Curved leaves keep their connected
  grid, and raised relief retains its denser surface.
- Layer masks use exact binary decisions and nearest sampling. Masked cutouts have no soft Gaussian
  backing that could protrude past their silhouettes.
- Image-file charts and text use graphic classification instead of unconditionally running photo
  inference. Graphic masks use the raised picture's detail, and image crops are reused.
- Cache accounting includes actual buffer allocations, source canvases, and retained relief maps. A
  discarded prefetch is attempted once per request, rather than rebuilt repeatedly when installed
  pages fill the cache. The Picture lab prepares its next PDF page on demand; the book retains
  lookahead.
- Your book, Picture lab, and Photo album opt in. The Storybook and Picture frame retain their
  existing rendering; GIF and video sheets retain their live screen path. The three changed toys
  identify their pages as hybrid in their input notes.
- The final resolution cap remains reachable when zooming a supersampled page, even if its last
  detail step is smaller than the normal rebuild threshold. The unchanged phone zoom spec caught and
  verified this behavior.
- Replaced faces and their GPU resources are destroyed with their sheets. Switching toys hides their
  faces immediately while the existing retirement queue releases the old resources.

The album carries over sharp continuous photo faces, bounded Gaussian backing, connected page
motion, exact cutout silhouettes, and cleanup. PDF text extraction and ink-paper processing do not
apply to its image set. Its order, aspect grouping, mounts, corners, captions, cover materials,
navigation, and heavier pull behavior are retained.
