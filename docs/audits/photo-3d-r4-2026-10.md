## What remains in the code

Source-only color, Gaussian weight, and edge-topology preparation runs in a worker while the
unchanged depth model runs in its own worker. The depth-dependent gather reuses the original
weights, tap order, and floating-point division. A 64 MiB limit bounds the gather tables, including
source indices and weight sums; an unsuitable or stale cache uses the ordinary construction path.

A sibling image covers the canvas while the picture is being prepared. Covered draws pause, while
updates, input, and explicit captures continue. The cover stays until the replacement Sharp scene
has drawn. Retired GPU resources are released after actual draws, since a covered update also emits
a frame-end event. The image and 3D camera keep their original resolution and pose.

Surface construction writes identical typed buffers without allocating small arrays for every quad.
Background construction skips depth bins that cannot write a donor. The depth worker imports the
small pixel helpers directly instead of loading the reconstruction graph merely to resize an input.
No replacement depth weights, inpainting model, external runtime, or network service ships. The
original depth model remains 27,258,801 bytes and loads only for an actual conversion.

## Fill and fringe diagnostics

Directional streaking is the nonnegative excess of local structure-tensor coherence in newly
revealed pixels over nearby visible background. The report also retains gradient energy, the flat
fraction, and pixel support; smoothing away texture must not be mistaken for believable
reconstruction. This measure cannot establish that invented hidden content matches an unseen real
scene.

Thin anchors come from the source image and checkpoint depth. Foreground color must be supported
along a narrow structure with background on both sides. Fringe measures integrate excess transition
width in CSS pixels. Both the original score and the score that samples the local rendered
background are retained, with missing structures and uncertain backgrounds reported. Unsupported
cases remain null. The forest has only five supported anchors from 27 candidates; this is not
exhaustive hair or leaf coverage. Synthetic fixtures distinguish extrusion from isotropic texture
and penalize a blurred or missing wire.

The existing hole and edge-halo measures are unchanged. The original text regression remains a
required browser check. The encoded clips' 12 fps is not a product frame-rate measurement.

## What was rejected

- Source patch synthesis and lighting extrapolation as a broad default: source-colored streaks and
  seams remained on natural scenes.
- Source-supported silhouette expansion, overlap ownership, and source-alpha recovery: useful on
  controlled wires, but not consistently better on natural edges. Their production APIs and tests
  added during this run were removed together; no checkpoint test was removed.
- Neural inpainting with successive mask and context changes: directional streaking often fell
  substantially, but generated foreground echoes changed the apparent background beside wires. For
  example, the bicycle's worst phone fringe rose from about 0.879 to 1.028 CSS pixels in one broad
  survey. Removing the new silhouette renderer did not eliminate all halo regressions.
- Moving color work from fragment to vertex shaders: byte-identical sampled images, but worse GPU
  execution cost on a desktop plant scene. The renderer was restored.
- A position-input derivative of the same depth weights: exact tested outputs, but no useful speed
  gain when combined with the final cache. The extra model and positional table were removed.
- Quantized LaMa: visibly invalid checker patterns. Full-precision LaMa was too costly for this
  phone experiment. Neither model ships.

A stricter wall-lighting experiment is still under evaluation. Its controlled gradient fixture
improves while a checker texture rejects the fit. That is not evidence of a real-sample improvement.
