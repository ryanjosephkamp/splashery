## What changed

Both still-photo views now share a depth-aware background image. A worker extends the far depth
under foreground boundaries, finds eligible nearby far-side color donors, and copies their actual
RGBA pixels. It does not average the exposed color. The fill is inferred from this one picture; it
cannot recover the actual hidden scene.

Sharp picture gives each surface cell its own vertices, cuts at depth and layer discontinuities, and
antialiases the cut edges. A conservative background field keeps its geometry behind the foreground
throughout the staggered depth animation. The filled background uses regular texture coordinates
instead of stretching donor coordinates across a triangle. The outer boundary receives projected
coverage while retaining the farther depth, removing the narrow uncovered ribbon.

Fine Splats uses larger, slope-aware photo-textured footprints, explicit border anchors, filled
background splats, and a farther floor. It also draws a continuous filled background sheet. This is
a **hybrid**, labeled in the view note and status, and in the clips. The foreground remains Gaussian
splats. One color per splat, editing tools, Hands-on, and Look effects use the splats. An ordinary
added clay splat retains its own color and can extend beyond the photo boundary.

A custom upload detected as planar keeps an affine plane, raw area-sampled colors, and one depth
animation band. Depth noise therefore does not bend text or split a planar page while it rises. Both
shader languages use the same photo/background stream encoding. The background atlas is released
when its material is removed and disabled when the source becomes Moving photo or live input. Moving
photo retains its original relief shader and grid path.

Photo decoding, orientation, white transparency compositing, geometry preparation, depth fill, and
backing-field construction run in workers where supported. A flat photo preview appears while the
geometry is prepared, and splat emission yields between small clouds. The quiet border-depth filter
is prepared once in the worker, and the still-photo shaders omit unused clip branches. Superseded
geometry requests and late source decodes cannot replace the current photo.

Task 37's model and worker, task 38's clip functions, dependencies, samples, saved-scene schema, and
renderer version are unchanged.

## Exports and scene saves

The view picker identifies Fine Splats as **hybrid** beside the two view choices. Its continuous
background sheet is a display surface in addition to the foreground and background Gaussian splats.
Gaussian splat files contain those splats and their per-splat colors; they do not contain the sheet
or the original photo texture used by Fine Splats. Picture and video exports capture the displayed
view, including the sheet when it is active. A tool, Hands-on, or a Look effect uses the splats
instead, including in an effects export.

Scene JSON keeps the toy's settings: the sample choice, depth, detail, view choice, seed, camera,
look, and effects. It rebuilds the photo's geometry and the hybrid sheet when the sample loads; it
does not serialize those meshes or textures. An opened photo's bytes are not embedded in a scene
save. Reopen that photo after loading its scene in a fresh page. The toy's About help now states
these differences. Export and scene-save behavior itself is unchanged.

## Measurement method

The tool uses real Chromium renders at a fixed 390×844 drawing buffer and CSS size, device pixel
ratio 1, WebGL2 through SwiftShader, the low profile, no ground or effects, and a pure-green clear
color. Each sample is measured at Depth 0.2, 0.5, and 1, turned left, right, up, and down. The 20°
acceptance number is the sum of those twelve poses **per sample and view**, rather than a claim that
every individual pose improves by 80%. The 10° and 30° sweeps are sensitivity checks.

A 128-point projected photo boundary follows the actual quiet edge depth. Paired scanline intervals
retain concavities, with two pixels excluded on each horizontal boundary. This avoids counting
outside the picture as missing content. It also means thin defects within that excluded boundary are
not counted. The clear-color threshold is RGB `<5, >250, <5`; exact source-color collisions are a
limitation of a color-key measurement.

Backing is measured separately in a diagnostic render: it is magenta, the foreground is black, and
the original geometry, sorting, alpha, and UV clipping remain. Dominant magenta pixels
(`>210, <60, >210`) count strong backing exposure. Before, that exposed backing has no hidden-scene
fill. After, it carries the inferred background image. Exposure after the change is therefore not a
missing-pixel claim; both numbers remain in the downloadable tables. Partial alpha mixtures below
that threshold are excluded. The Splats diagnostic includes its hybrid background sheet.

Sharp tears are a rendered geometry/UV proxy: a surface depth gradient over two edge-cut thresholds
per cell, or a backing UV stretch above 3×, collapse below one third, or fold. Splats tears count
projected splats with aspect ratio above 3 and diameter at least 12 pixels. These are reproducible
proxies, not a perceptual score or knowledge of what was hidden in the original scene. The Splats
baseline has zero under this definition, so retaining zero is a nonregression, not evidence of an
80% reduction from a positive baseline.

## What did not help

- Increasing splat size alone left uncovered depth-edge and border pixels.
- A depth-aware donor image without changing backing geometry still exposed stretched UV bands.
- Raising a coarse backing rim covered gaps but could bring it in front during the tap. That
  override was removed; farther depth with projected boundary coverage works better.
- A flat or convex-hull measurement mask counted outside concave turned boundaries. A stale photo
  texture also corrupted an early comparison. Those runs are excluded from the tables.
- In the original study, mip blur, extra resampling, and more sharpening did not solve text
  registration by themselves. The text repair combines a shared projection with a cubic footprint. A
  shared plane alone helped the added font case but lost small-stroke detail in the default font.
- Removing the hybrid sheet or changing bitmap decoding did not fix the added font regression.
- Worker geometry alone did not fix a long first shader compile. Cached quiet depth and specializing
  the still-photo shaders removed the expensive unused branches.
- CPU Canvas2D contexts and avoiding a preview copy still produced intermittent 218–303 ms native
  first paints. The supported path now receives a preview Blob and original-RGBA ImageBitmap from
  workers, avoiding that large main-thread paint. Bitmap reuse, retirement, and generation fallback
  have browser regressions.
- A cache key using unrounded slider decimals sometimes rebuilt the surface on the main thread.
  Normalized depth and the profile are now part of the key.

## Limits and what was cut

The hidden-color fill is a nearest eligible donor approximation, capped at a 320-pixel long side;
newly exposed texture can look patchy or repeat, and a scene without a useful far-side donor still
lacks authentic hidden content. Larger angles and extreme depth can show this approximation. The
hybrid sheet is a deliberate coverage tradeoff, and exported/editable Gaussian data has no
continuous sheet. One color per splat is outside the Fine-view acceptance measurements.

The cached R8 quiet depth matches the 25-tap convolution at texel centers before quantization;
interpolation between centers is an approximation. Physical-device performance, total GPU memory,
wide-angle reconstruction beyond 30°, a perceptual tear score, generative inpainting, new depth
models, and clip reconstruction are outside this change.
