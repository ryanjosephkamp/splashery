## Notes

- Why the narrower resonance width (100 cents, the plate's is 150): the cube's modes at √8 and 3 are
  only 102 cents apart, and at 150 every note there rang both about equally.
- The nearest mode always rings at the sound's full loudness (the others relative to it on the
  curve), so a note between two modes rings both and no note rings nothing (the owner's October 2
  complaint about the plate at a narrow width). A real cell driven between its modes would ring
  weakly; the toy instead plays the nearest resonances, said in the About as "the mode nearest that
  pitch rings".
- (0, 1, 0) and (0, 2, 0) are taken alone (not mixed with their permutations): the transducer under
  the cell drives the vertical modes most. (0, 2, 2) mixes its three permutations, as the toy's
  mixed modes do.
- The brief says the real cell rings at megahertz; a 1 cm cell rings at tens to hundreds of kHz
  (megahertz is for the sub-millimeter channels of lab chips). The toy keeps its 1 cm cell and its
  real frequencies.
- `strongest()` (a frame's voiced pitch or loudest band) is a 15-line copy of studio.js's, not an
  import: studio.js imports chladni-3d.js, and importing back would make a cycle that breaks when
  chladni-3d.js loads first (the Node tests).

- The clip tool takes over the toy's Track on every frame (its clock is the clip's): a first try
  read the track once, before the toy held it, so the song ran on the wall clock and the clips
  played two to three times too fast.
- Physics seen in the clips: on a held note the beads first gather on the nodal surfaces, then drift
  along them toward where the water's velocity swings most (the Gor'kov potential's second term,
  −(3/2) f2 |∇φ|²/k²). That's real, and it was there in Live r8 too.
